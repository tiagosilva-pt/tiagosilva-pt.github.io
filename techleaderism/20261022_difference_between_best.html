What’s the difference between the best and everyone else?

22/10/2026

I’ve recently read the famous book "Atomic Habits" by James Clear. This is a book that brings awareness to practices and habits that most of us have but never really thought about. But there are also some very good insights about how we handle habits and repetitive tasks. The best takeaway I got from this book is how the power of repetition and the resistance to boredom seem to be the great difference between the best and everyone else.

At some point in the book the author has a conversation with a coach who had trained thousands of athletes, including several elite athletes, and asks "what’s the difference between the best athletes and everyone else?" The coach started with the obvious response that included the physical state, work ethic, genetics and mindset. But then pointed to the real differentiator being just habits: "at some point it comes down to who can handle the boredom of training every day, doing the same lifts over and over and over."

Elite athletes face the same boredom everyone else does, they get tired of doing the same exercises multiple times a week, every week, they also wake up some mornings with zero desire to go to the gym. But in the end what ultimately makes the real difference is the commitment to show up regardless of the mood. The beginnings are always exciting and physical strength and raw talent produce rapid gains, but in the long run the willingness to repeat the exact same exercises when the fun and excitement is gone is what makes the difference between the best and everyone else.

The coach’s definition of success based on fighting boredom and repeating over and over again the same exercises clashes with the Hollywood version of success pictured with high energy music and dramatic breakthroughs. The same happens in other activities outside of sports. The hero developer that fixed a bug brilliantly has spent thousands of hours solving bugs. The always confident leader has hundreds of decisive meetings and 1-1’s behind them. The team that launched a product seamlessly trained the same patterns of QA, testing and validation over and over again.

Inspiration is great to get started, but repetition is key to achieving excellence.
